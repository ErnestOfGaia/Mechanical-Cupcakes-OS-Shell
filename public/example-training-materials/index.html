On the line, whoever works the station teaches the station. One card is posted at the station each day, so the trainee and the teacher both know what to work on when they are not making food. The aids live in the full-page station binder.

How to print

Chrome or Edge · backgrounds on

Open the page, press Print

Use the Print button on the page, the Print link above, or Ctrl+P. Cards come out Letter landscape; the quick guide, the Teacher's Aids and the character sheet come out Letter portrait. That is set on the page, not by you.

Check two settings

Paper: Letter. "Background graphics": on, so the black banners and hazard stripes print. Leave margins on the default and scale on 100%.

Cut, laminate, punch

For the binder's half-sheet cards, print "2 pages per sheet" and cut the sheet in half, or trim a single landscape print. Laminate in 5 mil matte. Punch the left edge: the card keeps a 1.75 rem strip clear for the holes.

Save as PDF works the same way: choose "Save as PDF" as the printer and hand the file to a print shop. One card, one PDF.

Learning science: why it's built this way

Each one teach one · unfinished

Every choice on these pages is answering something. This section says what, and where the answer came from, so anyone can argue with it. It leans on two bodies of work: the 1944 Job Instruction manual, which is where "I do, we do, you do" and the four-step method come from, and Zingerman's, because they are a real food business that publishes its own training method instead of selling a theory.

Each one teach one

On this line, whoever works the station teaches the station. Nobody can promise that cook has ever trained anyone. A new hire stands next to somebody and is expected to pick up the recipes in three days; everything else, the good habits and the bad ones, gets learned by watching and copying without knowing why people do what they do. The materials don't replace that method, because it isn't going anywhere. They give it a script, a record, and a reason.

Three design choices, and what's behind each one

The teacher gets a book too

The best performer on a station is usually not the best teacher: once a job goes automatic you stop seeing the details a beginner needs, and teaching is a separate skill that can be taught. So every card has a Teacher's Aid, and the aid carries the method instead of assuming it.

Say the why, then make them say it back

Job Instruction's third step has the learner do the job, then do it again explaining each key point out loud, and the instructor decides when they know it. That's why every aid has ask-don't-tell pairs short enough to land between tickets, and why the sign-off is about what the trainee said, not just what they did.

One card a day, then it comes down

One card, one topic, one side. Days 1 to 3 are supervised; days 4 and 5 are unsupervised and verified, which is Job Instruction's fourth step: put them on their own, name who they go to for help, check often, and taper the coaching instead of stopping it.

What we're reading from Zingerman's

  • 1The Training Compact. Both sides are 100% responsible, not 50/50. The trainer agrees to document what's expected, provide the training and recognise performance; the trainee takes responsibility for their own training being effective. It's what turns "ask questions" from a personality trait into a stated duty.
  • 2Why the Best Performers Aren't Always the Best Trainers. The argument for a teacher's edition, made by an operator rather than a consultant.
  • 3The Essential Guide to Staff Training. Four stages of competence, and a usable definition of the word trained: consistently doing the job correctly with conscious effort. Not yet automatic, and not measured in speed.
  • 4The Manager's Role in Staff Training. The manager's job is not the teacher's job. Set the context, coach before, debrief after, and make sure the new skill gets used within two days.
  • 5Liked Best, Next Time. A two-prompt debrief where the trainee speaks first on both. Cheap enough to run at the end of a shift.
  • 6Recipes for Organizational Success. Teach it, define it, live it, measure it, reward it. They admit defining ought to come first and answer that without committing to teach, nobody ever makes the time to define.
This section is unfinished, on purpose. What's here is the reasoning behind the design as it stands today. The full comparison against professional practice, including where the evidence is thin and what it does not support, lives in the project's research notes and hasn't been written into this page yet. Take a copy with you

Runs on your own machine · nothing leaves the building

The binder is a folder, not a service. Download it, drop it wherever the office already keeps shared files, and open it. No account, no install, and no internet needed once you have it: the fonts and the stylesheet are inside the folder, so a card looks the same with the network unplugged.

Download the folder

One zip file. It holds all five cards, all five Teacher's Aids, the quick guide and the character sheet, in English and Spanish.

Unzip it anywhere

A shared drive works, so the whole crew reads the same copy. So does the office desktop, or a USB stick taped inside the binder.

Open the home page

It opens in whatever browser is already on the machine, and prints from there exactly the way this page prints.
